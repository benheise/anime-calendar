Guide

This calendar shows when new anime episodes release on Crunchyroll, HIDIVE, Netflix, Hulu, Prime Video and Disney+ in North America. It covers Summer & Fall 2026 and tracks 151 titles: new simulcasts, streaming premieres, and series continuing from earlier seasons.

Reading the schedule

  • Each entry is one release: the moment a subtitled episode goes live on the platform. That is usually 30 to 90 minutes after the episode airs on Japanese TV.
  • Releases are grouped into hour rows, so the whole week lines up by time of day. Today's column is highlighted, a line marks the current time, and releases that are already out are dimmed.
  • Select any entry for the show's details, its upcoming episodes, where to watch it, and a file you can add to your own calendar.
  • On a phone, pick a day from the strip above the schedule.
  • Up next lists the next seven days in order with a countdown to each release. All shows lists every title by status: airing now, starting soon, date not announced, or finished.

Times and time zones

  • Times are shown in your device's time zone. To use a different one, or a 24-hour clock, use the clock button at the top of the page.
  • Most simulcasts are tied to Japan's broadcast clock, and Japan doesn't observe daylight saving time. When US clocks fall back on the first Sunday of November, those releases move one hour earlier on US clocks, and one hour later when clocks spring forward in March. Releases scheduled on a US clock, like Netflix's all-at-once drops at midnight Pacific, keep the same local time. The calendar handles both automatically.
  • When a release time hasn't been published yet, the entry says Time TBA and is listed on its US Eastern date.
  • With JavaScript turned off, the page shows a snapshot of the week it was built, in Eastern Time.

Episodes, premieres and finales

Episode numbers count from the season's first episode. The second half of a split season shows no numbers unless its starting episode is known. A run ends after its announced episode count; if no count has been announced, the calendar assumes a 12-episode season and labels the last one as a likely finale. Recap weeks and broadcast breaks are skipped, so a show can miss a week and still finish on the right date.

  • PremiereFirst episode of a new series or season.
  • ReturnsFirst release of the second half of a season.
  • FinaleLast episode, from an announced episode count or end date.
  • Likely finaleLast episode if the season runs a standard 12 episodes. The count hasn't been announced.
  • All 10 episodesThe whole season releases at once.
  • FilmA feature film or one-off special.
  • Time TBAThe date is known but the release time isn't.

Platforms

Each release carries its platform's colour and name. Platform TBA means the show is announced for the season but its North American streaming service hasn't been confirmed. Some shows stream on two services at once; selecting either platform's filter includes them.

Shows that stream only on services outside these six, such as YouTube channels, OceanVeil or Ani-One Asia, aren't listed.

My list and calendar files

  • Star a show to add it to My list, then turn on the My list filter to see only those shows. My list and your settings are saved in this browser only.
  • Add to calendar downloads an .ics file with every upcoming release of that show, for Apple Calendar, Google Calendar or Outlook. Export my list does the same for every starred show. Calendar apps don't update these files on their own, so download a fresh copy when the schedule changes.

Where the information comes from

  • Platform announcements: Crunchyroll's seasonal lineups and news, HIDIVE's simulcast lineup, and Netflix, Prime Video and Disney+ listings.
  • Release times: AnimeSchedule's tracked subtitle release times, checked against releases as they happen.
  • Episode counts and endings: Anime News Network, official sites and Wikipedia episode lists.
  • Cross-platform checks: the Anime UK News simulcast line-up thread, What's on Netflix and What's on Disney Plus.

Schedules change often: delays, recap weeks, early drops, double episodes. Check the platform before a premiere.

How it's kept current

The schedule is re-checked against these sources regularly, and whenever a release doesn't match what was observed on the platform.
